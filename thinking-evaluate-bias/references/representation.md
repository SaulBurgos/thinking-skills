# Representation Bias

Use this lens to check whether the work's framing, structure, abstraction, model,
examples, or selected population distorts the problem or the conclusion.

## Keep It Separate

Representation is about **how reality is modeled and weighted**. A work can use
correct facts while framing the problem too narrowly, choosing an unsuitable
abstraction, treating one case as universal, or giving affected groups too
little weight.

If the work instead applies unequal scrutiny to competing beliefs, use
confirmation. If it leaves out a whole material fact, consequence, argument, or
perspective, use omission.

## Starting Questions

Use these as starting points, not a complete checklist. Adapt, skip, or add
questions based on the work and decision.

- How does the work define or frame the problem?
- What model, categories, abstractions, or boundaries shape the analysis?
- What does that representation make visible, and what does it hide?
- Does the framing assume a cause, solution, or preferred outcome?
- Does it confuse a proxy, average, or simplified model with the underlying reality?
- Does it treat one case, environment, or common pattern as universal?
- Do the people, systems, cases, environments, examples, or data represented match what the conclusion covers?
- Are affected groups or edge cases underweighted relative to their impact?
- Would a reasonable alternative framing materially change the analysis?

## When to Report It

Report a material finding only when:

1. you can identify the framing, model, abstraction, or represented group;
2. it differs from the relevant reality or target in an important way; and
3. the difference could change or materially qualify the conclusion.

Name the distorted representation, explain what it hides or overweights, and
give the smallest useful alternative framing or evidence needed to test it.

## Examples

- Frame a retention problem only as employee motivation while excluding workload and management structure.
- Treat a dashboard metric as the outcome itself even though it is only a proxy.
- Test only enterprise accounts, then recommend the solution for every customer.
- Check a migration only in development, then assume production will behave the same way.
- Describe what decision-makers need while underweighting the operators affected by the decision.

Do not force demographic or stakeholder diversity into work where it cannot
affect the reasoning or decision.
