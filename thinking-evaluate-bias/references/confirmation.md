# Confirmation Bias

Use this lens to check whether the work favors a preferred conclusion through its framing, research, evidence standards, or treatment of other options.

## Keep It Separate

Confirmation is about **uneven scrutiny**. The facts may be correct and the request may be complete, but the preferred option can still get deeper research, a kinder interpretation, or an easier standard of proof.

Do not call it confirmation bias just because one conclusion has better evidence. Fair treatment does not mean every option must end in a tie.

## Starting Questions

Use these as starting points, not a complete checklist.  Adapt, skip, or add questions based on the work, evidence, and context to fit the work.

- Does the question already assume the answer it wants?
- Did the work look for both supporting and conflicting evidence?
- Did it seriously check other plausible explanations or solutions?
- Were other options rejected through assumptions while the favorite was tested with evidence?
- Were counterexamples minimized, explained away, or held to a tougher standard?
- What evidence would prove the preferred conclusion wrong, and was it checked?
- What is the strongest believable case against the recommendation?
- Would reversing the framing of the question produce a very different analysis?

## When to Report It

Report a material finding only when:

1. you can identify a preferred conclusion or hypothesis;
2. competing options received meaningfully different levels of scrutiny; and
3. using the same standard could change or qualify the conclusion.

Explain the uneven treatment and name the smallest useful disconfirming test or counterargument. Present strong alternatives honestly. Do not invent weak opposition just to make the answer look balanced.

## Examples

- Explore why a migration will succeed without checking the strongest failure case.
- Profile only the preferred database while dismissing other options through undocumented assumptions.
- Treat supporting stories as evidence but reject conflicting stories as unrepresentative.
- Investigate the first suspected cause without testing other explanations that fit the same symptoms.
