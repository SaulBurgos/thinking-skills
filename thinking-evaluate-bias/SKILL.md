# Thinking Evaluate Bias

## Goal

Evaluate work for reasoning and structural bias through three lenses:

1. **Representation**
2. **Confirmation**
3. **Omission**

## Workflow

Identify:

- the problem, question, or decision;
- how it is represented or framed;
- the apparent preferred conclusion;
- the evidence and alternatives considered; and
- material information or perspectives left out.

For a full review, apply all three lenses:

### 1. Representation

Check whether the framing, abstraction, model, examples, or selected population
distorts the problem or makes the conclusion appear broader or stronger than
the analysis supports.

Read and apply [Representation](references/representation.md).

### 2. Confirmation

Check whether the work searches for, interprets, or evaluates evidence in a way
that favors an existing belief or preferred conclusion.

Read and apply [Confirmation](references/confirmation.md).

### 3. Omission

Check whether critical facts, consequences, counterarguments, alternatives, or
perspectives were excluded in a way that favors a conclusion.

Read and apply [Omission](references/omission.md).

Complete all three lenses before reaching an overall conclusion.

For a focused review, apply only the requested lens and clearly state that the
other lenses were not evaluated.

## Boundaries

- Do not turn the review into a general accuracy, completeness, or contextual-fit audit.
- Use factual evidence only to demonstrate or reject a possible bias pattern.
- If a bias finding depends on a key fact, quote, or citation, check an independent
  or primary source. For quotes or citations, confirm the source exists and
  supports the attribution.
- Check only claims that could establish, reject, or change the finding. If a
  claim cannot be verified, label it unverified and do not treat it as conclusive.
- Do not report every missing detail as omission; it must materially tilt the conclusion.
- Assign overlapping findings to the lens that best explains the distortion.
- Mention secondary effects without reporting the same finding twice.

## Output

For each evaluated lens, report:

- whether material bias was found;
- the framing, preference, or missing information involved;
- evidence supporting the finding;
- how it could affect the conclusion; and
- the smallest useful balancing action.

Finish with how the assessed bias findings affect the original conclusion:
no material change indicated within the checked scope, qualification needed,
reconsideration needed, or insufficient evidence to assess the effect.
Do not imply that this review establishes the conclusion's overall correctness.

Stay read-only unless the user asks for changes. Never claim that work is
bias-free; say that no material bias was found within the checked scope.
